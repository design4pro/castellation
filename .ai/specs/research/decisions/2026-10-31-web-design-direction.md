# Web design direction - owner decisions

- Date: 2026-10-31
- Owner: Product owner, the developer participating in this discovery session.
- Source: Direct answers in the Castellation product discovery and visual-direction brainstorm conversations.
- Scope: Website style and design. This record does not authorize implementation.

## Stated assessment

The owner considers the website as a whole insufficiently modern and professional, describing its current appearance as generic AI-generated design and reminiscent of the 1990s. This is the owner's assessment, not a reader interview, independent visual audit, or measured usability finding.

## Confirmed choices

1. The immediate brief concerns the website's style and design.
2. The website should evoke a library, preferably an old library.
3. Literary characters and relationship graphs take priority over books and their contents.
4. When asked to distinguish a contemporary interface with an old-library atmosphere from a literal library interior used as the interface, the owner selected the contemporary interface with an old-library atmosphere. The alternatives were supplied by the agent; the owner confirmed the selection.

## Approved visual direction

On 2026-10-31, the owner selected and subsequently approved **Relationship Atlas** as Castellation's visual direction, refining D01 through D03 in the product brief:

- The relationship graph is the main visual motif of Castellation.
- Character portraits are prominent elements inside the graph, not limited to character profiles.
- Typography and color create the old-library atmosphere within a contemporary interface.
- Characters and their relationships remain primary; books provide context. Relationship readability and spoiler boundaries remain constraints.

The agent also proposed Character Index and Reading Room After Dark, plus retaining the current prototype without redesign. The owner favored Relationship Atlas because the graph would define the visual identity while typography and color would convey the library atmosphere. The owner explicitly requested prominent portraits when offered a primarily typographic graph.

The owner confirmed ending the brainstorm at this stage. No follow-up skill, screen production, specification, or implementation was authorized. This update records that decision only.

## Limits and unresolved details

No reader accounts, audience research, visual references, usability measurements, commercial targets, or implementation schedule were supplied. Exact color values, font families, portrait illustration technique, cropping, frames, and interaction details remain agent proposals rather than approved requirements. The owner's assessment does not establish that readers share it. The existing website is a prototype; its implementation and internal design reports establish previous choices and behavior, not demand or visual effectiveness.

Readability of a dense graph with prominent portraits, particularly on mobile, and the quality, completeness, and consistency of available portraits remain unverified. A representative desktop/mobile graph walkthrough was proposed as a future check, not authorized work. Existing chapter boundaries apply to portraits as well as character and relationship information.

## Consequences

The approved Relationship Atlas direction guides future design discussions instead of the existing bookshop-oriented direction. It does not update the implemented design or authorize changes to it. Exact visual treatments and evaluation criteria remain proposals until approved. Revisit this direction with the product owner when evaluating design concepts or when reader evidence conflicts with it. Any change to these choices requires owner confirmation and a superseding record.
